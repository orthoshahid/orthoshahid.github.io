Knee Replacement: A Patient Guide

Knee replacement may be considered for selected people with severe knee arthritis when pain and functional limitations persist despite appropriate non-surgical care. The decision is individual and follows a clinical assessment.

BengaluruPatient educationIndividual assessment matters

When might knee replacement be discussed?

People with persistent pain, stiffness or loss of function that affects everyday activities may wish to discuss options with an orthopaedic surgeon. Imaging, symptoms, examination, health and personal goals all matter.

What alternatives may be considered?

Depending on the diagnosis, options may include activity modification, physiotherapy, suitable medicines, weight management where relevant, walking aids or other treatments. Not every treatment is appropriate for every person.

What happens during recovery?

Recovery commonly involves pain management, safe mobilisation, wound care and progressive exercises. Timelines vary. Follow your own surgical team's instructions for wound care, driving, work, exercise and follow-up.

Risks and informed decisions

Knee replacement is major surgery. Potential risks can include infection, blood clots, stiffness, persistent pain, implant-related problems and anaesthetic complications. Ask your surgeon about individual risks, benefits and alternatives.

Important: This page provides general education only. It does not diagnose a condition or replace an examination by a qualified clinician.

Prepare for a consultation

Bring relevant imaging, reports, a list of medicines and questions about your symptoms and goals.
