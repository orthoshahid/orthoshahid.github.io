How Do Fractures Heal? Stages, Recovery and Aftercare

A fracture is a break or crack in a bone. Healing begins soon after the injury, but the time needed to regain strength and function varies with the bone involved, fracture pattern, blood supply, age, general health and treatment. A cast, brace or operation may be recommended depending on the injury.

The four broad stages of bone healing
1. Early responseBlood clot and inflammation start the repair process.
2. Soft callusRepair tissue bridges the fracture area.
3. Hard callusNew bone forms and gradually strengthens the bridge.
4. RemodellingBone reshapes over time in response to healing and loading.

What affects how quickly a bone heals?

  • Fracture type and location: some bones and fracture patterns heal more slowly than others.
  • Stability and blood supply: appropriate alignment, stability and circulation help repair.
  • Smoking and nicotine: these can delay bone healing. Avoid smoking and discuss support for quitting.
  • Nutrition and health: adequate food, protein and a balanced diet matter. Conditions such as diabetes or certain medicines may affect recovery.
  • Following the treatment plan: putting weight on the limb too early or removing a splint or cast without advice may cause problems.

Why are follow-up appointments and X-rays important?

Your orthopaedic team may arrange follow-up examinations and X-rays to check alignment and signs of healing. An X-ray is interpreted alongside pain, tenderness, function and the type of fracture. Do not judge healing from a single image or compare your X-ray with someone else's: the plan and expected timeline are individual.

How can you support recovery?

  • Keep the cast, splint or wound cared for exactly as instructed.
  • Attend scheduled reviews and ask when it is safe to move the joint, bear weight, drive or return to work and sport.
  • Eat a balanced diet with sufficient protein and calcium-containing foods; ask a clinician if you may need vitamin D or other assessment.
  • Do prescribed exercises or physiotherapy at the recommended stage. Avoid unapproved exercises or heavy loading.
  • Do not start high-dose supplements or stop prescribed medicines without professional advice.

When should you seek urgent medical attention?

Get urgent help if pain or swelling becomes severe or rapidly worse, fingers or toes become numb, cold, pale or blue, you cannot move them, a cast feels excessively tight, there is new discharge or fever after surgery, or you develop chest pain or sudden breathlessness. An open fracture or a major injury needs emergency assessment.

Will every fracture need surgery?

No. Many fractures can be treated with a cast, splint or brace. Surgery may be needed when a fracture is displaced or unstable, involves a joint, damages surrounding structures, or cannot be kept in a suitable position without fixation. The decision depends on examination, imaging, overall health and the person's needs.

Remember: healing times vary, and pain settling does not always mean the bone is ready for unrestricted activity. Follow the plan given by your treating clinician.
